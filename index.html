Middle East O&G Maintenance Customer Experience

Disclaimer: Scope excludes Egypt

Executive summary

Hempel is mapping the maintenance customer journey to understand how the ecosystem works, where customers face friction across the asset lifecycle, and where Hempel can strengthen its position in the maintenance market.

The objective is to move beyond a transactional supplier relationship and identify how Hempel can become a more proactive, trusted maintenance partner-from early planning, inspection and specification to contractor selection, procurement, application and post-maintenance follow-up.

Research approach

The project combines internal perspectives from Business Development and Sales with interviews across customer roles, including commercial management, project engineering and operations. It traces who influences decisions, where responsibilities shift, and when Hempel can offer the most useful support.

Saudi Arabia is a key focus of the research, while several interviewed customers also manage projects across the wider Middle East, including Bahrain, Qatar and other markets. Egypt is considered separately because its market structure and maintenance business dynamics differ, and is excluded from this scope. Early findings guide further validation; they are not regional benchmarks.

Key findings so far

  1. Approval is necessary, but it does not guarantee selection. Asset owners determine which coating systems are approved. Contractors often choose among eligible suppliers based on price, availability, technical support and existing relationships.
  2. Maintenance projects carry uncertainty. Unpredictable material requirements, changing schedules and supply disruptions create operational and financial risks for contractors.
  3. Build on technical expertise and customer relationships. Customers value both, while pointing to opportunities in pricing flexibility, product availability, application practicality and specialist response times.
  4. Engage before the bid. Customers expressed interest in more proactive collaboration, access to historical maintenance information and help preparing competitive proposals.
  5. Adapt to how the work is bought and executed. The journey changes when the asset owner purchases directly versus delegating responsibilities to contractors.

What we aim to achieve

  1. Map the end-to-end journey, including stakeholders, responsibilities, decisions and Hempel touchpoints.
  2. Identify and prioritize unmet needs and points of friction.
  3. Find ways to engage earlier and support customers beyond coating supply.
  4. Align Business Development, Sales, Solution Management, Technical Service, Supply Chain and other relevant teams around a shared view of the journey.
  5. Build a prioritized improvement roadmap to support retention, competitiveness and new maintenance business.

Expected outcome

The intended deliverables are a validated end-to-end customer journey, a consolidated view of needs and pain points, and a prioritized set of opportunities. These will inform cross-functional decisions and help Hempel move from reactive, project-by-project interactions toward ongoing engagement across the maintenance lifecycle.

Our ambition: Make Hempel easier to work with, more valuable to maintenance customers, and better positioned to win and retain Oil & Gas maintenance business.

Key strategic conclusions - Middle East

01 · Technical approval opens the door, but does not secure the sale

Hempel needs relationships both with asset-owner specifiers who determine technical eligibility and with contractors who make purchasing decisions.

02 · Earlier engagement can strengthen maintenance opportunities

Better coordination between Business Development Managers, Sales and technical teams could help Hempel engage eligible contractors before tender decisions are finalized.

03 · Commercial and operational reliability drive contractor preference

Competitive pricing, dependable material supply and responsive technical support influence whether contractors continue choosing Hempel among approved alternatives.

Overall takeaway: Across the Middle East maintenance context examined, the challenge is not simply gaining specification approval. It is turning technical eligibility into contractor preference, project orders and long-term maintenance relationships.

EXTERNAL CUSTOMER PERSPECTIVE

Executive summary

Maintenance success depends on more than coating performance. Contractors face strict asset-owner specifications, uncertain work volumes, competitive tenders, supply constraints and tight execution windows.

  1. Create value before award. Help with approvals, estimates, market intelligence and bids. One commercial interview explicitly requested proactive partnership.
  2. Compete on price and deliver reliably. Approval and price matter in planned work; when downtime is at risk, stock, delivery certainty and fast problem resolution can outweigh price.
  3. Build on strong relationships without overlooking process gaps. Customers praised local responsiveness and expertise, while citing gaps in approvals, pricing flexibility, application practicality and specialist turnaround.

Hempel can move from responding to coating orders to supporting maintenance planning, execution, performance monitoring and the next maintenance cycle.

What is working well for Hempel

Customers highlighted strong relationships with local teams, quick answers to technical questions, willingness to visit sites, and clear communication during supply disruptions. Two customer accounts linked the responsiveness of Hempel’s people to supplier preference or effective problem resolution. Preserve these strengths as the team improves pricing, supply and specialist handoffs.

Important journey variations

The map is a reference, not a fixed sequence. Procurement route changes who owns the handoffs; urgency changes what customers prioritize.

Contractor-led maintenance

The contractor manages estimating, quotations, materials and execution; the asset owner retains technical and contractual authority.

Asset-owner direct purchasing

Large operators may buy approved coatings directly and distribute them through their warehouses. This route is informed by internal evidence, not validated by direct asset-owner interviews.

Shutdown and urgent repair

Short timelines increase the need for pre-mobilization readiness, available stock and rapid technical support. Where used, a letter of intent may allow material preparation to start early.

Planned versus urgent maintenance

Planned

More time to estimate, compare and schedule: price competitiveness, approved product quality and application productivity.

Urgent

Less time and greater operational risk: immediate stock, fast delivery and response, and approved alternatives.

4. Three journey variations to preserve

Journey A - Contractor-led maintenance

Asset owner identifies work → Contractor estimates and bids → Asset owner awards the contract → Contractor purchases from Hempel → Contractor executes the work → Asset owner accepts the work.

This is the principal journey described across the external contractor interviews. The detailed acceptance process still needs confirmation.

Journey B - Direct purchasing by the asset owner

Asset owner identifies material requirements → Asset-owner Procurement orders directly from Hempel → Hempel delivers to central warehouses → Materials are distributed to facilities → Contractor performs maintenance.

This route was described in the internal Saudi Business Development interview, especially for centrally purchased maintenance materials.

Journey C - Contractor-led assessment and scope definition

Asset owner recognizes a need → Contractor inspects and assesses the asset → Contractor proposes the technical scope and resources → Asset owner approves → Tendering or procurement and execution proceed.

This variation applies where the asset owner delegates more responsibility for technical assessment and planning.

Strategic takeaway

The biggest customer frustrations arise when technical, commercial and operational requirements are not aligned.

Customers need products that are approved, competitively priced, available when required and supported throughout application. Delivering on only one of these dimensions is not always enough to secure or retain maintenance business.

Hempel's priority should be to remove friction at the critical handoffs between specification, tendering, procurement and execution, while building more proactive relationships that help customers plan future maintenance work.

Credit readiness is a cross-stage issue: customers experience the block at ordering, but it can be prevented through earlier Sales and Credit Control alignment during tendering and commercial discussions.

1. Our strategic ambition

Transform Hempel from a responsive coating supplier into a proactive, trusted maintenance partner, supporting asset owners and contractors throughout the maintenance lifecycle.

Our ambition is to use the findings from internal and external interviews to identify where Hempel can create greater customer value, remove friction from critical interactions, strengthen relationships and unlock sustainable growth in the Oil & Gas maintenance market.

This means moving beyond individual coating transactions towards a more integrated approach that connects technical expertise, commercial support, material availability and long-term asset performance.
